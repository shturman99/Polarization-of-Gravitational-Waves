## 5. Referee verdicts

- **Importance: USEFUL** (upper edge), not significant as it stands. The
  factorisation + band-split is a reusable negative result; the f′(0⁺) criterion is
  a real economy. But the advertised headline restates what is already encoded in
  the LISA/PTA turbulence templates in use today, and no forecast number is given.
- **Novelty: publishable, but the abstract is inverted** with respect to what is new.
- **Necessity: MAJOR REVISION + RESTRUCTURE.** Three papers stapled together.

### Where the referees converged without seeing each other

1. Claim 6 is the real contribution and must be made **quantitative** — §VII promises
   two calculations and performs neither.
2. The **highest-leverage next step** is the same for both: run the existing
   `Notebooks/finite_coherence_gw.py` down to p ≪ 1/τ̂ and plot the IR slope crossing
   **3 → 1** at p ≃ 1/τ_c. This exhibits a UETC that *does* produce k¹, and shows the
   peak-pinning cusp result and the k¹ result are **the same mechanism** — currently
   treated in two disconnected sections.
3. The manuscript **explains the simulations away** rather than explaining them: it
   never runs its own kernel at RP20's T_em, τ_c and k-range.

### Factual correction to the manuscript (Referee A)

§V stated the 0.15–3 mHz band *"is where LISA is most sensitive."* **Wrong** — LISA's
Ω-sensitivity minimum is 3–10 mHz, essentially at the peak. The k³-vs-k¹ stake is
overwhelmingly **PTA**: the whole PTA band sits 1–2.5 decades below the peak, so it
measures nothing but the IR tail, and NANOGrav 15 yr gives f^1.3–2.4 — the two
hypotheses straddle the data. — FIXED: the band is now correctly placed *below* the
minimum, the consequence reframed as parameter reconstruction rather than
detectability, and the PTA stake stated.

## 5b. Second pass — commentary and framing polish

A follow-up pass rewrote the surrounding prose so the manuscript's own claims match
what the reviews established.

**Abstract** — was internally inconsistent: it asserted the IR is set by the spatial
model alone, then two sentences later described a finite-duration k¹ regime that moves
it. Now qualified to *asymptotes* ("every intermediate feature, the peak and the break
alike, responds to both"), "choice of temporal model" narrowed to "choice of
**stationary** temporal model", the cusp claim given its Auclair conditional, and the
closing dissipation claim marked as a gap identified but not closed.

**All five conclusions rewritten.** (1) factorisation restricted to asymptotes, with
`Cai:2019cdl` credited for the IR half and the aeroacoustic precedent for the UV half;
(2) white-noise floor credited to `Caprini:2006jb`/`Caprini:2009fx`/`Brandenburg:2019uzj`,
band-split reframed as the sharper test they did not run; (3) Ω_T ∝ Ω_M removed
entirely, replaced by the band statement plus an explicit admission that the coherence
assumption is unproven and assuming it is circular; (4) the ω⁻² tail conceded as the
standard Abelian theorem, keeping only the *peak-location* corollary as new, with the
magnetic-UETC conditional; (5) dissipation flagged as "a programme rather than a result".

**Other commentary fixes.** The k_break attribution now credits `RoperPol:2022iel` for
the break itself, keeping only the box-fundamental comparison as ours. `Auclair:2022jod`
is now also cited as *support* for the finite-lifetime mechanism (their
constant-then-switched-off source), not only as the objection that threatens it. The
§VI k⁻¹-vs-k⁻²ᐟ³ point credits `Niksa:2018ofa` for raising it first. §VII now states
plainly that the subviscous calculation **has not been performed**, and warns that
"several decades of k" is a bandwidth statement, not an energy statement — the
Kulsrud–Anderson picture concentrates subviscous energy near the resistive scale, so
the contribution may be small.
